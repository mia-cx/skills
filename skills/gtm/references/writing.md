# Writing

Write posts and short copy that get read and acted on: one reader, one action, a first line that earns the second, a shape a phone skimmer can follow, and an ask at the end. Sentence-level style follows [talk-normal](../../talk-normal/SKILL.md). For Mia, her voice follows [talk-like-mia](../../talk-like-mia/SKILL.md), and where it conflicts with a formatting rule here (bullets, lists, length), talk-like-mia wins.

## 1. Plan the post

Write three lines above every draft, because everything in the post serves the one action:

```
Reader: <the one reader from the copy sheet's positioning, or the lead>
Action: <one of read, reply, share, follow, save, click, buy>
Takeaway: <one sentence the reader walks away with>
```

- **One idea per post.** One idea is easier to remember and pass on, and on X it keeps the post inside one interest cluster ([x.md](x.md)). Three ideas make three posts.
- **Advanced idea, simple words.** Keep the concept intact and the vocabulary readable by a 12-year-old.
- **Pass the magician test.** A layperson should find it interesting and an expert ten times more so. Copy written for a general audience still lands with experts, but copy written only for experts loses everyone else.
- **Tell the change from the user's side.** Find the moment things became different for someone. The user is the main character and the product is what helped. When no real change exists yet, ask the user for one instead of writing a story without it.

Done when the three lines are filled in and every sentence of the draft serves them.

## 2. Write the body, then the hook

Write the body first, because the hook can only promise what the post delivers. Then write 5–10 hooks using different types, cut the weakest at once, refine the survivors until each line rolls into the next, and keep one. Hand the user the best two.

A hook's only job is to get the next line read. It opens a gap the reader wants closed and makes it about them:

- Lead with the most interesting thing. If the best line sits at the bottom, move it to the top.
- Be specific: numbers, names and timeframes. "I hit 5.8M impressions in my first month" beats "I grew fast".
- Pull at least one lever (self-interest, curiosity, novelty, emotion, identity) and stack two or three where they fit. "If you're a founder under 1K followers, this one change doubled my reach" is identity, then self-interest plus curiosity.

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Bold claim | "Copywriting is the most underrated skill on X." |
| Callout | "If you're a founder with under 1K followers, read this." |
| Curiosity gap | "I just realized something 99% of founders miss." |
| Clear outcome | "How to write posts like the top 1%." |
| Contrarian | "You need to post less." |
| Story | "It took me 8 years to realize this..." |
| Number or list | "7 copywriting rules I wish I knew 6 years ago." |
| Proof | "This post got 2M views. I analyzed why." |

Use only numbers the user gave you or the copy sheet holds. Place the hook where the format needs it:

```
Short post   → the hook is the post; line one stands alone
Thread       → hook plus a promise of what's coming ("Here are 7 lessons:")
Article      → title, subtitle and first paragraph each hook on their own
Video        → first 1–2 seconds: movement, on-screen text, spoken hook
Quote post   → the take adds tension or value to the original
Image        → the headline on the image, one idea, readable at phone size
```

## 3. Shape it for skimmers

Pick a shape before writing, because a post without one reads as a ramble: problem → insight → takeaway; a list (hook, numbered points, closer); a graphic; a video; an article; a quote post. Show proof and credibility as early as possible.

The last line is the second most important line after the hook. Close on one of three: the takeaway in 1–2 lines, a question, or a tease of what's next.

Most readers scan on a phone before they read:

- Keep paragraphs to 1–3 lines; four lines look like an essay on a phone.
- One thought per line. A sentence with two "and"s and a comma is probably two sentences.
- Use line breaks, numbers and arrows so a scanner picks up the point.
- Add a connector line where readers drop off ("Let me explain...", "But here's what nobody tells you:").
- Say "you" more than "I".

Done when the draft has a shape, an early proof point, a firm last line, and no paragraph over three lines.

## 4. Ask for one thing

Readers tend to do what a post asks, so end on one low-effort ask: the action from step 1. Match it to how well the reader knows the user, because asking a stranger for a sale is a big jump:

| Reader | Ask for | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doesn't know the user yet | A follow or a share | "Send this to a friend who's building a brand." |
| Trusts them a bit | A reply, a save or a click | "Which one do you disagree with?" / "Full breakdown in the article below." |
| Ready | A sale, a DM or a sign-up | "DM me 'system' and I'll send you the details." |

The end isn't the only place for the ask. Put one early too, where most readers still are. Propose posting it as a reply under the user's best-performing post, and try asks that invite the reader in: their advice, or what they want covered next.

## Voice

Hooks and formats can be copied; how the user sounds can't. Drafts sound like the user talks, hold their opinions, and use what actually happened to them. For anyone other than Mia, get a five-line voice card before drafting: how they want to come across (smart, funny or blunt), two opinions others in their field don't share, one true story, one quirk to keep. Store it in `.gtm/voice.md`.

AI drafts always look polished, so polish says nothing about quality; the bar is whether the user would sign their name to it:

- Every draft holds lines a person would say out loud, zero jargon, and at least one number or example from the user's notes.
- Invent no result, client name or number. Missing material becomes a question for the user.
- Before handing over, edit like a reader: mark the line where a reader would stop, and fix it or say so.

When the user publishes an edited version of your draft, compare the two. Once a week, propose the five edits they made most often as new lines for `.gtm/voice.md` (for Mia, as proposed changes to talk-like-mia), so the next drafts need less rewriting.
