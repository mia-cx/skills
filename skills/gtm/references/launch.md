# Launch

A launch spreads when strangers have something to add when they quote it. Plan who shares it, and what each will say, before it goes live.

## The post

Show the product on one real case: what went in, what came out, what still needed fixing by hand. Test it with: what could someone who has never heard of the user add? If nothing, add specifics. Claims come from the copy sheet.

Plan 3–5 angles others can quote it from:

- **Origin**: the problem that led the user to build it.
- **Implication**: what changes for one specific job.
- **Reaction**: someone trying it on camera, including where they are skeptical.
- **Take**: a defensible claim with evidence to argue against.
- **Tension**: who gains and who loses.

Pick contested angles only when the team can answer the pushback with evidence.

## The people

For each angle, find people who live the problem, starting from the scouts' accounts and leads. Read their posts and replies, and record why their audience would care; follower count alone qualifies nobody. Brief each one:

```markdown
What it does: <one line from the copy sheet>
Try it yourself: <access, demo account, or sample input>
Why your audience might care: <their angle, tied to their posts>
Evidence: <link>
What's your take after trying it?
```

They write in their own words; paid partnerships are disclosed. Sending briefs follows the main skill's posting rules.

Ready when: one claim with evidence, angles matched to briefed people with access, an owner for questions and for business follow-ups, and a clear way to try the product.

## Launch day and after

Draft answers where each question was asked: under the post, its quotes, and creators' posts. Log recurring questions, objections, bugs, and surprise use cases in `.gtm/launch.md` with links, and draft a reply to whoever raised an issue once it ships. Feature someone's demo only with permission and credit.

Judge angles and people by trials, relevant DMs, and sales calls, not views. Recurring questions become FAQ entries, walkthroughs, and landing copy. Bring back the people who started useful conversations with something new: a deeper walkthrough, a customer case, an update.
