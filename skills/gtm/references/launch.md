# Launch

A launch post spreads when strangers have something to add when they quote it. Plan who will share it, and what each of them will say, before it goes live.

## The post

Show the product working on one real, specific case: what went in, what came out, and what still needed fixing by hand. Before publishing, answer: what could someone who has never heard of the user add to this? If nothing comes to mind, add specifics until something does. Every claim comes from the copy sheet, with its evidence.

Plan 3–5 angles other people can quote the post from:

- **Origin.** The problem that led the user to build it.
- **Implication.** What changes for one specific job, and why one feature matters more than it first appears.
- **Reaction.** Someone trying it on camera, pausing on what surprised them and where they are skeptical.
- **Take.** A specific, defensible claim with enough evidence to argue against.
- **Tension.** Who gains and who loses: saved money for one person is a threat to another's business.

Choose contested angles only when the team can answer the pushback with evidence, because the debate decides what people end up believing about the product.

## The people

For each angle, find people who live the problem: practitioners, specialists, customers. Start from the scouts' niche accounts and leads. Read their posts and replies first, and record why their audience would care; follower count alone qualifies nobody.

Draft a short brief per person:

```markdown
What it does: <one line from the copy sheet>
Try it yourself: <access, demo account, or sample input>
Why your audience might care: <their angle, tied to their posts>
Evidence: <link>
What's your take after trying it?
```

They write in their own words, and paid partnerships are disclosed. Sending briefs follows the main skill's posting rules.

Ready to launch when every item holds: one claim with evidence, angles matched to people, those people briefed and given access, a named owner for questions and for business follow-ups, and a clear way to try the product or get in touch.

## Launch day

Draft answers in the thread where each question was asked: under the user's post, under quotes, and under creators' posts. Log recurring questions, objections, bugs, and unexpected use cases in `.gtm/launch.md` with links. When a fix or requested feature ships, draft a reply to the person who raised it. Ask permission before featuring someone's demo or objection, and credit them.

## Next wave

Judge angles and people by the trials, relevant DMs, and sales calls they brought, not by views. Turn recurring questions into FAQ entries, walkthroughs, and landing-page copy. Bring back the people whose posts started useful conversations, with something new to explore: a deeper walkthrough, a customer case, or an update that answers their audience's questions.
