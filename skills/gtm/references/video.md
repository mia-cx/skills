# Product video

Two jobs share this file: a polished product video, and short-form posts built on proven formats. An AI-generated film is in [ai-film.md](ai-film.md). In all of them, claims on screen come from the copy sheet, and the user watches motion and listens to sound, because you can do neither.

## Product video

A video looks expensive through restraint: every choice is deliberate, and nothing is there by accident.

1. **Rules.** Fix them per video type (launch, feature, explainer) before production: about 3 colors by hex, one background style, one typeface, one kind of music. Reuse them across videos so the brand reads as decided.
2. **Storyboard.** Design every frame before animating. One idea per shot, the key object centered, room to breathe, and a background that never competes with it. Center the product when it is the subject; when people carry the story, place heads at 38% or 62% of frame width.
3. **Motion.** Ease and overlap every keyframe; linear motion reads cheap. Move between scenes with transitions, cutting hard only when the message needs it, and vary the pace across sections.
4. **Music.** Pick tempo for the feeling, then a genre for the audience: 60–80 BPM cinematic, 90–110 smooth, 115–123 sharp and premium, faster reads as hype.
5. **Sound.** After the edit, have the user listen through, and cut every effect that is too loud, out of place, or does not help the viewer understand the product.

Show the storyboard to the user before animating, because frames are cheap to change and animation is not.

## Short-form

Platforms push formats they already recognize, so reuse a proven format and keep everything else the user's own. Take the structure: the opening, the beats, the framing. Subject, words, footage and claims stay the user's, because lifted scripts or clips are plain copying.

Find formats through outliers: videos that beat their creator's usual views. A big account's views mostly reflect its audience; the gap shows the format did the work. Favor creators under about 100,000 followers with several breakouts, since their formats fit small accounts.

1. **Teardown.** For a video the user sends, get its transcript (on Instagram, the caption), top comments and numbers against the creator's recent videos. Return: the first line verbatim, each beat in order, what viewers came for, what commenters keep asking, what to keep and swap for this product, 5 hooks from different angles, and claims to verify. Mark why-it-worked as opinion, since the data only shows that it did. Save the structure to `.gtm/templates.md`.
2. **Voice.** Once, study the user's 30 latest captions and any transcripts. Save their go-to words, words they avoid, how their videos open and close, and AI-sounding habits to keep out to `.gtm/voice.md`. For Mia, start from [talk-like-mia](../../talk-like-mia/SKILL.md).
3. **Scripts.** From a voice note, build 3 scripts on 3 templates, using only the user's words and claims: first line, on-screen text, beats, ending and caption. Check them against `.gtm/voice.md`, save the user's edits back to it, and log the chosen script in `.gtm/log.md`.
4. **Review.** Weekly, compare the week's videos with the user's 3-month baseline. Name the 3 strongest and 3 weakest and what the strong ones share (hook, format, length, topic), state the sample size behind every pattern, and call anything under 6 videos early. Compare saves on TikTok only. Add 3–5 lessons to `.gtm/lessons.md`.

One practitioner runs these as scheduled bots on Virlo, a paid viral-video database; any source of transcripts, comments and per-video views works.
