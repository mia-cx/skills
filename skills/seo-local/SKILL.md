# Local SEO

Part of [seo](../seo/SKILL.md): its evidence method and boundaries apply here.

Get a business that serves customers in person into Google's map pack by auditing its Google Business Profile (GBP) against the businesses that already rank there. Google ranks local results on relevance, distance, and prominence; the profile is where the business controls relevance and part of prominence. Changing the profile is an account change, so propose edits and apply them only with the user's go.

## Check eligibility first

GBP requires in-person contact with customers, at a staffed location or within a service area. An online-only business does not qualify: say so and route the work to [seo-content](../seo-content/SKILL.md). A virtual office or remote mailbox breaks the [GBP guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) and gets the listing removed. A service-area business clears its address from the profile and lists up to 20 service areas instead.

## Listings

- Verify the profile, because Google says verified businesses are more likely to show in local results.
- Use the business's real-world name, with no keywords added, because the guidelines treat extra words in the name as grounds for suspension.
- Pick the most specific primary category that fits. Keep name, address, phone, and hours accurate and identical everywhere they appear: Google says complete, accurate profiles are more likely to show up, and Whitespark's 2026 expert survey ranks website NAP matching the profile 15th and business hours (open at search time) 5th.
- Link the profile to the matching service page, with a UTM tag so calls and bookings from the profile are measurable. Fill in contact and booking details, real job photos, and current offers.
- List the business only where local customers look: Apple Business, Bing Places, Yelp, and the country's main directories from [Whitespark's citation sources by country](https://whitespark.ca/top-local-citation-sources-by-country/). Submitting listings needs the user's go.

## The audit

The step order and weekly pace follow one local SEO consultant's method; run the steps in that order, one per week. The prompts need an agent that can browse; without one, ask the user for screenshots of each listing. Ranking weights below come from Google where it says so and otherwise from Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 local SEO experts, which is opinion, not measurement.

1. **Load context.** Write one file with the business name, address, phone, website, GBP URL, service areas, and target keywords; one file per map-pack competitor for each "[service] in [city]" search, with its GBP URL; and the current GBP categories, attributes, photos, and services. Done when every map-pack competitor for every target search has a file.
2. **Categories and attributes (week 1).** Categories decide which searches show the business on the map: Whitespark's experts rank the primary category 1st and additional categories 8th of 187 factors. Add a category only when it completes "this business IS a…", not "HAS a…", because the guidelines ask for the fewest categories that cover the core business and forbid categories used as keywords. A service the business offers goes under services instead.

   ```
   Search '[service] in [city]' on Google and note which competitors show in the Map Pack. Open each competitor's GBP listing and extract their primary and all secondary categories. Spreadsheet, one tab per keyword. Columns: business name, primary category, secondary categories, star rating, review count, ranking position. Highlight categories competitors have that my GBP lacks.
   ```

   Look for patterns, such as every business ranking for "emergency plumber in [city]" also listing "water damage restoration", then keep only the categories the business is.
3. **Services and description (week 2).** List every real service from the website on the profile; Whitespark's experts rank GBP predefined services 22nd, and the consultant reports services missing from GBP lose those map searches.

   ```
   Go to my GBP listing at [URL] and these competitors: [URL1], [URL2], [URL3]. For each, extract every listed service, whether it has a description, how detailed it is, and any groupings. Spreadsheet comparing my services section with theirs. Then list services on my website [URL] missing from my GBP. Finally, write a 2–3 sentence description for each of my services that names the service area where relevant and states a benefit. Services: [list]. Areas: [list].
   ```
4. **Reviews (week 3).** Track count, rating, and pace together. Google says more reviews and positive ratings help local ranking; Whitespark's experts also rank review recency 11th and a steady flow over bursts 14th. The consultant's example: 90 reviews gaining 15 a month beat 200 mostly two years old.

   ```
   Go to these competitor GBP listings: [URL1], [URL2], [URL3]. For each, read the last 50 reviews and extract: total count, average rating, reviews in the last 30/60/90 days, most mentioned services, most mentioned neighbourhoods or cities, recurring complaints. Compare their review velocity with mine at [my GBP URL]. Separate tab: how many reviews per month I need to catch the top competitor, and how long it takes.
   ```

   Then audit the last 30 owner responses on each listing (response rate, speed, tone, handling of negative reviews) and draft reply starters for 5, 4, 3, and 1–2 star reviews. Replies are for customers reading them, not for ranking: Google says replies show you value feedback, and Whitespark's experts rank owner responses 122nd and keywords in them 182nd of 187. Follow Google's reply guidance: short, personal, conversational rather than promotional, and focused on reviews where the reply adds an answer, an update, or a fix; reply promptly to negative reviews without sharing private details.
5. **Posts and photos (week 4).** Posts help customers choose more than they help ranking (Whitespark: post frequency 148th), and Google archives posts after 6 months unless a date range is set. Audit competitors' posts from the last 90 days (count, type, images, calls to action, topics), then draft an 8-week calendar; the consultant posts 2–3 times a week: seasonal offers, before-and-after jobs, neighbourhood posts ("just finished a kitchen remodel in [neighbourhood]") rotated across 8–10 areas a month, review highlights, team spotlights. Write the first 4 weeks in full with image suggestions; posting needs the user's go.
6. **Routine (week 5 on).** Post on the calendar, upload real photos weekly, and reply to new reviews.

## Review rules

Google's review policy bans paying or rewarding customers for reviews, discouraging negative ones, and asking only happy customers (review gating), and it bars merchants from requesting specific content. Draft a request the user sends to every customer after each job, with the profile's review link or QR code, asking for an honest review, with no suggested wording for the review itself.
