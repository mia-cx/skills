# Local SEO

Part of [seo](../seo/SKILL.md): its evidence method and boundaries apply here.

Get a business that serves customers in person into Google's map pack by auditing its Google Business Profile (GBP) against the businesses that already rank there. Google ranks local results on relevance, distance, and prominence; the profile is where the business controls relevance and part of prominence. Changing the profile is an account change, so propose edits and apply them only with the user's go.

## Check eligibility first

GBP requires in-person contact with customers, at a staffed location or within a service area. An online-only business does not qualify: say so and route the work to [seo-content](../seo-content/SKILL.md). A fake address or virtual office breaks the [GBP guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) and gets the listing removed. A service-area business hides its address.

## Listings

- Use the business's real-world name, with no keywords added, because the guidelines treat a stuffed name as a violation.
- Pick the most specific primary category that fits. Keep name, address, and phone identical everywhere they appear.
- Link the profile to the matching service page, with a UTM tag so calls and bookings from the profile are measurable. Fill in contact and booking details, real job photos, and current offers.
- List the business only where local customers look. Candidates: Apple Business, Bing Places, Yelp, Better Business Bureau, Nextdoor Business, the local Chamber of Commerce, Foursquare, Manta, Brownbook, EZlocal, Hotfrog, CitySquares, Cylex, Cybo, MerchantCircle, Superpages, DexKnows, Yellowbook, Alignable. Submitting listings needs the user's go.

## The audit

These steps follow one local SEO consultant's method and run in order of impact, one per week. The prompts need an agent that can browse; without one, ask the user for screenshots of each listing. Ranking claims in this section are that consultant's view, not Google's.

1. **Load context.** Write one file with the business name, address, phone, website, GBP URL, service areas, and target keywords; one file per map-pack competitor for each "[service] in [city]" search, with its GBP URL; and the current GBP categories, attributes, photos, and services. Done when every map-pack competitor for every target search has a file.
2. **Categories and attributes (week 1).** Categories decide which searches show the business on the map, and adding a missing secondary category is often the fastest win. Add only categories that describe the business, because the guidelines ask for the fewest categories that cover it.

   ```
   Search '[service] in [city]' on Google and note which competitors show in the Map Pack. Open each competitor's GBP listing and extract their primary and all secondary categories. Spreadsheet, one tab per keyword. Columns: business name, primary category, secondary categories, star rating, review count, ranking position. Highlight categories competitors have that my GBP lacks.
   ```

   Look for patterns, such as every business ranking for "emergency plumber in [city]" also listing "water damage restoration".
3. **Services and description (week 2).** A service on the website but missing from GBP is invisible for that map search.

   ```
   Go to my GBP listing at [URL] and these competitors: [URL1], [URL2], [URL3]. For each, extract every listed service, whether it has a description, how detailed it is, and any groupings. Spreadsheet comparing my services section with theirs. Then list services on my website [URL] missing from my GBP. Finally, write a 2–3 sentence description for each of my services that names the service area where relevant and states a benefit. Services: [list]. Areas: [list].
   ```
4. **Reviews (week 3).** Track review pace over star rating, because recent reviews weigh more than old totals: 90 reviews gaining 15 a month beat 200 mostly two years old.

   ```
   Go to these competitor GBP listings: [URL1], [URL2], [URL3]. For each, read the last 50 reviews and extract: total count, average rating, reviews in the last 30/60/90 days, most mentioned services, most mentioned neighbourhoods or cities, recurring complaints. Compare their review velocity with mine at [my GBP URL]. Separate tab: how many reviews per month I need to catch the top competitor, and how long it takes.
   ```

   Then audit the last 30 owner responses on each listing (response rate, speed, tone, handling of negative reviews) and draft response templates for 5, 4, 3, and 1–2 star reviews, three variations each, naming the service and place where it fits. Reply to every review.
5. **Posts and photos (week 4).** Audit competitors' posts from the last 90 days (count, type, images, calls to action, topics), then draft an 8-week calendar at 2–3 posts a week: seasonal offers, before-and-after jobs, neighbourhood posts ("just finished a kitchen remodel in [neighbourhood]") rotated across 8–10 areas a month, review highlights, team spotlights. Write the first 4 weeks in full with image suggestions; posting needs the user's go.
6. **Routine (week 5 on).** Post on the calendar, upload real photos weekly, and reply to every review.

## Review rules

Google's review policy bans paying or rewarding customers for reviews and asking only happy customers (review gating). Draft a request the user sends to every customer after each job, asking for an honest review, with no suggested wording for the review itself.
