# LinkedIn

Part of [gtm](../gtm/SKILL.md): its copy sheet and boundaries apply here.

Get the user's posts, articles and messages in front of buyers on LinkedIn. It suits writers and products sold to businesses. Posts come from [gtm-writing](../gtm-writing/SKILL.md) and videos from [gtm-video](../gtm-video/SKILL.md); this skill covers what changes on LinkedIn. You research and draft; the user posts and sends everything at a human pace, because LinkedIn's terms prohibit third-party automation of messaging and it restricts accounts that behave like automation.

## Posts

- Post from the user's own profile, not a company page, because early on a founder's account drives more growth than a brand's ([gtm-organic](../gtm-organic/SKILL.md)). On a team, each person's profile is a channel.
- Rebuild each piece for LinkedIn rather than pasting another platform's version. In the five-minute chain, the LinkedIn post comes after the video and the X thread, and each central idea later becomes its own post.
- LinkedIn groups count as communities: the user takes part before sharing a link, as in [gtm-reddit](../gtm-reddit/SKILL.md#take-part-before-the-link).

## Articles that rank

When LinkedIn articles hold page one for a query the user's new site can't rank for yet, Google is already happy to rank LinkedIn for it.

1. Search the query in a private window and confirm LinkedIn articles rank there.
2. Put the query early in the headline. The headline sets the article's URL, so check it before publishing.
3. Lead with the answer and an example, written for LinkedIn readers rather than copied from the user's blog.
4. Link to one next step on the user's site, and link to the article from the user's site.

Each article is a complete answer, because thin pages hosted to borrow a site's ranking count as [site reputation abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#site-reputation).

## DMs from competitors' ads

Open on a problem buyers already pay to hear about: one that three competitors all advertise this month.

1. Open linkedin.com/ad-library (no account needed). Ask the user for the 3 companies they lose deals to, and search each as advertiser, last 30 days.
2. For each ad, note the opening problem, the offer, and the job title it targets. Collect 5–10 ads on the problem all three share.
3. Draft 3 DMs in this shape, 2 lines and under 20 words, keeping the problem and dropping the ad wording, since a DM that reads like an ad gets ignored:

```
we helped [company like theirs] fix [the problem from the ads] in [how long]. want the breakdown?
```

The customer, fix and time must be a real result from the copy sheet.

Build the list one of two ways, and ask the user which:

- **Free.** LinkedIn's people search, filtered by the job title from the ads, plus company and location. Send each person a connection request; once they accept, send the DM. Free accounts can attach a note to only a few requests a month, so put the DM's problem line in those notes and send the full DM after acceptance.
- **Sales Navigator (optional, paid subscription).** Filter by job title, industry and company headcount, and message people outside the user's network with InMail credits. Confirm the current price with the user before suggesting it.

Propose a test: each DM to 50 people, then 3 new versions of the one with most replies after a week, then the rest of the list. On the free route, the test runs at the pace people accept requests. The user sends every message inside LinkedIn's weekly invitation and message limits. A weekly rerun of steps 1–3 is fine.

## Find people

Scouts reach LinkedIn through web search with `site:linkedin.com`, because its API refuses unauthenticated requests ([gtm-outreach](../gtm-outreach/SKILL.md)).
