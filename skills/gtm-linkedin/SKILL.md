# LinkedIn

Part of [gtm](../gtm/SKILL.md): its copy sheet and boundaries apply here.

Get the user's posts, articles and messages in front of buyers on LinkedIn. It suits writers and products sold to businesses. Posts come from [gtm-writing](../gtm-writing/SKILL.md) and videos from [gtm-video](../gtm-video/SKILL.md); this skill covers what changes on LinkedIn. You research and draft; the user posts, comments and sends everything at a human pace, because LinkedIn's User Agreement bans bots and unauthorised automation that send messages, add contacts or create, comment on, like or share posts, and bans scraping.

## Posts

- Post from the user's own profile, not a company page. In Metricool's 2026 study of 673,658 LinkedIn posts (vendor data), personal profiles had a 63% higher engagement rate than company pages at similar impressions per post. On a team, each person's profile is a channel.
- Rebuild each piece for LinkedIn rather than pasting another platform's version. In the five-minute chain, the LinkedIn post comes after the video and the X thread, and each central idea later becomes its own post.
- Keep the user out of engagement pods: LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies ban agreeing ahead of time to like or re-share each other's posts.
- LinkedIn groups count as communities: the user takes part before sharing a link, as in [gtm-reddit](../gtm-reddit/SKILL.md#take-part-before-the-link).

## Articles that rank

When LinkedIn articles hold page one for a query the user's new site can't rank for yet, Google is already happy to rank LinkedIn for it.

1. Search the query in a private window and confirm LinkedIn articles rank there.
2. Put the query early in the headline. LinkedIn builds an article's URL from its title, and editing the title later changes the URL (the old one redirects), so settle the headline before publishing.
3. Lead with the answer and an example, written for LinkedIn readers rather than copied from the user's blog.
4. Link to one next step on the user's site, and link to the article from the user's site.

Each article is a complete answer. Google's [site reputation policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#site-reputation) exempts the user's own posts on a platform built for user content, but its ranking systems still favour helpful, people-first pages over thin ones.

## DMs from competitors' ads

Open on a problem buyers already pay to hear about: one that three competitors all advertise this month.

1. Open linkedin.com/ad-library, LinkedIn's public database of ads run since June 2023. Ask the user for the 3 companies they lose deals to, and search each as advertiser, last 30 days.
2. For each ad, note the opening problem, the offer, and the job title it targets. Collect 5–10 ads on the problem all three share.
3. Draft 3 DMs in this shape, 2 lines and under 20 words (one practitioner's format). Keep the problem and put it in the user's own words, because LinkedIn's User Agreement bars copying content from the site without its owner's consent:

```
we helped [company like theirs] fix [the problem from the ads] in [how long]. want the breakdown?
```

The customer, fix and time must be a real result from the copy sheet.

Build the list one of two ways, and ask the user which:

- **Sales Navigator (paid subscription).** Filter by job title, industry and company headcount, and message people outside the user's network with InMail credits, LinkedIn's paid channel for messaging strangers. Every plan gets 50 credits a month, banking up to 150, and extra credits can't be bought; a reply within 90 days refunds the credit. Confirm the current price and the user's credit balance before suggesting it.
- **Free.** LinkedIn's people search, filtered by the job title from the ads, plus company and location. The user sends connection requests only where there is a real reason to connect, and the invitation itself carries no pitch, because LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies ban using invitations to send promotional messages to people you don't know. Free accounts can add a personal note to 5 requests a month. Once someone accepts, the user may send the DM.

In the UK, marketing email and social media DMs to individuals, sole traders and some partnerships need their prior consent (PECR). The ICO treats a DM to a profile on LinkedIn or another networking site as reaching an individual, because people there rarely act only for their employer. So on either route, the user DMs someone in the UK only with that consent. Full rules: [gtm-outreach](../gtm-outreach/SKILL.md#drafting-rules).

Propose a test: each DM to 50 people, and after a week, if one DM leads by a wide margin, 3 new versions of it, then the rest of the list. At a 10% reply rate, telling 10% from 15% apart takes about 576 sends per version (Evan Miller's sample-size rule), so close counts at 50 are a tie: report the pilot as inconclusive, then stop or plan a larger test. On Sales Navigator, the 150 sends need 150 credits, so fit the pilot to the user's balance: split it across months, or test fewer DMs. Either way, split each batch's leads at random across the DMs and count each send's replies over the same window, so no DM wins on a different audience or more time. On the free route, the test runs at the pace people accept requests. LinkedIn caps invitations without publishing the number and restricts accounts that hit the cap, typically for a week, so the user sends at a human pace. A weekly rerun of steps 1–3 is fine.

## Find people

Scouts reach LinkedIn through web search with `site:linkedin.com`, because its API requires authenticated access and its User Agreement bans scraping the site ([gtm-outreach](../gtm-outreach/SKILL.md)).
