# Organic content

Part of [gtm](../gtm/SKILL.md): its copy sheet and boundaries apply here.

Plan content that builds a brand: a body of work people explore after reading one post, from the user's own account, on a schedule they can keep. A viral post followed by silence means no brand exists; the test is whether anyone would ask where the user went if they stopped. Build from the scouts' evidence so each topic traces back to people who asked. Drafts follow [gtm-writing](../gtm-writing/SKILL.md); X specifics live in [gtm-x](../gtm-x/SKILL.md).

## 1. Write the brand document

Interview the user one section at a time and record answers verbatim in `.gtm/brand.md`, because their exact words, and their reader's words when complaining to a friend, are the language posts should use.

1. **Who they help.** One real person: a bad day, what they say when they complain to a friend, what they already tried.
2. **Their story.** What they lived through that the audience lives now, the moment things changed, what they built, failed at and figured out, the results they can point to.
3. **Who shaped their mind.** Books, posts and videos they never skip, the idea they keep returning to.
4. **Pillars and perspective.** What they could talk about for hours, the 2–3 that matter most, how they come at each differently ("as a former nurse"), what people ask their advice on.
5. **Mission and beliefs.** What they help people do, in one sentence; what they believe that most in the space don't; which common advice is wrong.
6. **What they're against.** A myth or habit, never a person or company. Reuse the "against" line from [gtm-foundations](../gtm-foundations/SKILL.md).
7. **Why now.** What just changed that most people haven't noticed.
8. **Where they take people.** The reader's day five years on, what they'd put in their bio, the user's role (guide, documenter, someone who came back with a map).
9. **Rules and words.** 5–12 rules the ideal reader lives by, and names for the user's own ideas and methods.

Then write `.gtm/influences.md`: for each of the 5–10 people named in section 3, read their 10–20 best posts per platform (books and talks when they post little) and note their core ideas, turns of phrase with personality, the worldview people rally behind, and what drove their growth. The user's angle is how they combine these people, so record what makes each distinctive and never clone a voice.

Done when all nine sections hold the user's answers or a marked blank, and the influences file covers each named person. Reread both files before every planning or drafting session, and update them when the user's story changes.

## 2. Map the content

A content map is a tree the user picks the next angle from, so they never start at a blank page:

| Level | Count | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pillars | 3–4 | Broad themes the user will own |
| Topics | 5–7 per pillar | Areas inside each theme |
| Ideas | 5–10 per topic | Specific points likely to perform |
| Angles | 4–5 per idea | Ways into the same idea |

1. **Demand.** Cluster verified leads by the words people use. A question that keeps coming up with no good answer anywhere is the strongest topic. Note what the niche accounts' best posts share and what their replies ask that the posts never answer.
2. **Performance.** Research which ideas and formats performed in those topics over the past 6–12 months. Include platforms the user doesn't post on, because ideas proven there are often unused on the user's own.
3. **Lens.** Choose pillars where demand overlaps with something the user knows unusually well or has lived, taken from the brand document. Each pillar cites its leads.

Lens alone gives original posts nobody asked for; research alone gives the posts everyone else writes. Take patterns (a structure, a hook shape, a format) and fill them with the user's ideas; never copy a post one-for-one. Done when every pillar cites leads and every idea names the pattern it borrows.

## 3. Pick channels and cadence

- **One platform first**, where the scouts found the audience, matched to the user's strength: writers to [X](../gtm-x/SKILL.md), [LinkedIn](../gtm-linkedin/SKILL.md) or newsletters; on-camera people to short video ([gtm-video](../gtm-video/SKILL.md)); talkers to podcasts and [YouTube](../gtm-youtube/SKILL.md). Each platform branch covers how pieces are shaped, posted and timed there.
- **The user's own account**, because early on a founder's account drives more growth than a company page or paid campaigns. On a team, each person's account is a channel.
- **A cadence they can keep for months.** Practitioners recommend 3–5 posts a week, or daily. Ask what time the user can commit and start lower; consistency beats volume. Payoff takes months.

## 4. Draft

Most posts help without selling: answers, how-tos, teardowns, build logs of what the user built and learned this week. A few convert: demos, launches, pricing. Mix written and video, short and long. You research and draft; the experience, opinions and results come from the user.

Write `.gtm/content.md`: the map with evidence, channel and cadence, where each piece goes after publishing and who might share it, and 5–10 first drafts labelled by pillar and funnel stage. Posting follows [gtm-outreach](../gtm-outreach/SKILL.md#posting-rules)'s posting rules.

## Content from conversations

Conversations with users and other builders supply material in the users' words instead of the company's, and one with a bigger account borrows its reach. Propose to the user:

- **The five-minute chain.** Record a 5-minute call with a user, post it as a video, turn it into an X thread, then a LinkedIn post, then pull each central idea out as its own post.
- **Power-user group chats.** Act on the feedback, then mine the chat for topics.
- **Case studies**, when the product sells to businesses, since they move buyers more than anything else. One interview becomes a write-up, a thread and a short clip.
- **Sales-call mining.** Read each new transcript and propose 5 post ideas, each tied to the line that sparked it, plus 3 things to fix on the next call (talked too long, skipped a question, went soft on price), each with its line. Contact nobody from a call.

Quote a customer or caller publicly only with their permission.

## Reshape per platform

The same article on five sites reaches nobody new. Rebuild each piece in its platform's native form: articles as [YouTube](../gtm-youtube/SKILL.md) demos and short clips that show the result at once; original findings as [Reddit](../gtm-reddit/SKILL.md) answers or answers to existing Quora questions; research reports as SlideShare decks or carousels with one answer per slide, captions and alt text; tutorials on DEV or Hashnode; design case studies on Behance or Dribbble; essays on Substack or Medium; DIY processes on Instructables. End each piece with a related guide, partner with complementary creators, and repeat the formats that bring enquiries. Each version is worth reading on its own, because rewording one thin article across many sites for links is link spam ([seo](../seo/SKILL.md)).

## Iterate

Each week, check for the four ways content fails: gaps between posts, missing conversations the audience is already having, no distribution plan, and posts readers struggle to follow. With metrics, make more versions of clear outliers and retire formats that underperform across several posts.
