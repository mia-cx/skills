# Earned links

A link is worth earning when the linking page's readers benefit from the destination. Pick tactics that fit the evidence the business has:

| Opportunity | What earns the link |
| --- | --- |
| Unlinked mention or reused asset | Ask for the credit; restore or redirect removed pages that still have referring links |
| Roundups and guides | A buyer need the article misses, with tested facts and a proposed addition |
| Original research | The site's own data, published with method, sample, dates, and limits at a stable URL |
| Journalist requests | A current request inside real expertise, answered first with a verifiable example |
| Competitor and broken links | Why the publisher cited that resource, and a replacement that serves the same reader |
| Free tools | A task the audience repeats, built only within the requested scope |
| Listings and vendor stories | An eligible listing customers use, or a real workflow with evidence |

Read every prospective page before recommending it, and claim no completeness from backlink data.

Track prospects in the project's existing tracker: source URL, relevant passage, destination, evidence, contact route, and status (discovered, verified, drafted, sent, published). Draft short messages naming the exact page and the proposed addition. Mark a link published only after checking it live, with its attributes.

Earn editorial citations only. Reciprocal-link schemes, bought links, required dofollow, and automated comment or directory posting violate [Google's link spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam). Paid placements carry `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow`, and commercial relationships are disclosed.
